Affiliate disclosure

Last updated: 20 September 2026 · Applies to: whichfoodtoavoid.com

In one sentence: some links on Whichfoodtoavoid are affiliate links — if you buy through one we may earn a small commission, at no extra cost to you, and it never buys a recommendation.

1. Affiliate links explained

An affiliate link is a tracked URL. If you click it and then buy, the merchant pays us a commission. The price you pay is identical whether you use our link or go direct. Our Amazon links carry the Associates tag faiza013-20.

2. Which programmes we take part in

  • Amazon Associates Programme — as an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.
  • Other merchant and network programmes (for example Awin, Digistore24, ClickBank and direct merchant schemes) where they are relevant to the subject of the page.

3. How this affects our content

  • Commission rates never decide what we feature, how we rank it, or what we say about it.
  • We do not accept payment for positive reviews, and we do not write sponsored posts here.
  • We say so when we have not tested something ourselves, and we rely on stated product specifications, published reviews and manufacturer documentation.
  • We mention drawbacks, and we update or remove recommendations when products change.

4. Our compliance position

This disclosure follows the UK Advertising Standards Authority (CAP Code) rules on recognising marketing communications, the Competition and Markets Authority's guidance on online endorsements, the US Federal Trade Commission's Endorsement Guides, and Amazon's own Associates Programme Operating Agreement.

5. Prices and availability

Prices and stock change constantly. Anything you read here is a snapshot at the time of writing — always check the merchant's own page before buying. Amazon prices in particular can change within hours.

6. Getting in touch

If you think something on this site is mislabelled, or you want to know whether a specific link is an affiliate link, email hello@whichfoodtoavoid.com.
