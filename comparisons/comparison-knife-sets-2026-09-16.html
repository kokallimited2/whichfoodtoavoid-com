❓ FAQs

Are either of these knife sets suitable for professional kitchen use?

Neither is designed for commercial kitchen demands, where forged German or Japanese knives from Wüsthof, Victorinox, or Global are the standard. The Cutluxe comes closest with its 56–57 HRC hardness and full-tang construction, making it a solid choice for passionate home cooks; the Astercook is firmly in everyday household territory.

How do I sharpen these knives at home?

The Astercook's stamped blades respond well to a pull-through sharpener or a basic whetstone — both are inexpensive and widely available in the UK. For the Cutluxe, a quality honing rod used regularly will maintain the 14–16° factory edge; reserve a whetstone for full resharpening every few months, and avoid aggressive pull-through sharpeners that remove too much steel.

Which set offers better long-term value?

The Astercook wins on upfront cost-per-piece at roughly $1.54 per item, but stamped blades typically need replacing sooner. The Cutluxe costs more now but its harder steel edges stay sharp longer, reducing the frequency of sharpening and eventual replacement — making it the stronger long-term investment for cooks who use their knives daily.
