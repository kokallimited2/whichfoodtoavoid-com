📝 User Reviews

Both models earn consistent praise for build quality and longevity, with many buyers reporting a decade or more of regular use. The Classic's most common criticism is vibration at high speed when kneading stiff or wholemeal doughs.

🏆 Verdict

If you bake at least once a week or make bread regularly, the Artisan earns its £100 premium through a stronger motor and a more practical bowl. For fortnightly bakers focused on cakes and biscuits, the Classic delivers the same fundamental result at a clear saving.

Artisan best for weekly bakers tackling bread, large batches, or anyone who wants the extra wattage and wide colour range. Classic best for occasional bakers who mainly work with cakes and cookies and want to keep costs down.

❓ FAQs

Do both mixers accept the same KitchenAid attachments?

Yes. Both share KitchenAid's standard power hub, so every KitchenAid attachment, including pasta rollers, food grinders, and spiralisers, fits either machine without adapters.

Does the Classic include a pouring shield?

No. The pouring shield is sold separately for the Classic, which adds to the total cost. The Artisan includes one as standard in the box.

Which handles bread dough better?

The Artisan, at 325W, kneads bread dough more comfortably. The Classic can manage standard white bread, but it vibrates more and runs hotter with dense wholemeal or enriched doughs.
