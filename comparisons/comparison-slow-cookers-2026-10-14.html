🏆 Verdict

If your goal is batch-cooking soups, stews, and casseroles once or twice a week, the Bella SAVOR does it well at £39.99 without asking you to read a manual. The Instant Pot Ultra makes sense if you cook under time pressure on weeknights, as it can get a beef brisket to the table in under 90 minutes rather than 8 hours, but only if you will genuinely use three or four of those 10 modes regularly.

Bella SAVOR best for budget-conscious households that slow-cook weekly and want a non-toxic, no-fuss single-purpose appliance. Instant Pot Ultra best for busy households that want pressure-cooker speed alongside slow cook, saute, yogurt making, and sous vide from one machine.

❓ FAQs

Is the Bella SAVOR's ceramic coating genuinely chemical-free?

Bella certifies the EverGood coating as free from PFAS, PFOA, PTFE, lead, and cadmium. No independent third-party lab certification is publicly listed, but those five exclusions cover the main health concerns around slow-cooker liners. Avoid metal utensils and abrasive sponges to preserve the coating.

Can the Instant Pot Ultra replace a dedicated slow cooker?

Not reliably. Multiple independent testers found that the Ultra's slow-cook Low setting runs at roughly the same temperature as a traditional slow cooker's Keep Warm mode, which means meals that need 8-10 hours can come out undercooked. For slow cooking specifically, the Bella SAVOR is the more dependable choice.

Which handles a large Sunday roast better?

Both pots hold 6 quarts, enough for a 1.5 kg joint with root vegetables. The Bella SAVOR gives a hands-off 8-hour cook on Low. The Instant Pot Ultra can pressure-cook the same joint in roughly 60-75 minutes, though the texture differs from slow-cooked results. Which you prefer depends on how much time you have, not which cooker is superior.
