UK buyers praise both machines for consistent daily smoothies and quick cleanup, though a number of NutriBullet owners flag gasket leaks and slow customer service responses, while Ninja users occasionally mention needing to shake the cup mid-blend when thick frozen chunks stick to the blade.

🏆 Verdict

At £59, the Ninja Fit is worth the extra £9 if you blend ice or frozen chunks most days, since its 700W motor and two included cups give you clear practical gains. The NutriBullet at £49.99 earns its place for anyone who needs a larger single serving and wants a blender that is even simpler to operate with no hands-on technique required.

Ninja Fit QB3001SS best for daily frozen-fruit blenders who want two cups and stronger ice-crushing power. NutriBullet NBR-0601 best for single-serve drinkers who want a larger 24 oz capacity and a lower upfront cost.

❓ FAQs

Can either blender crush whole ice cubes?

The Ninja Fit handles ice better at 700W, though both perform well when ice is broken into rough pieces first. The NutriBullet's 600W motor manages small chips but can stall on a full cup of large cubes.

Are the cups dishwasher safe on both machines?

Yes. Both use BPA-free cups that are dishwasher safe. The Ninja ships with two 16 oz cups; the NutriBullet includes one 24 oz cup with a to-go lid and a lip ring.

Which handles leafy greens better for green smoothies?

Both cope well with spinach and kale. The Ninja's six-pronged blade pulverises tough fibrous greens in about 20 seconds; the NutriBullet's extractor blade is comparably effective, and its larger cup means you are less likely to overfill when adding bulky leaves.
